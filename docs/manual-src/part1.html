Summary

At a glance

  • Sourcing › RFQs is a menu path: open Sourcing in the left-hand menu, then click RFQs.
  • Submit is a button or link exactly as labelled on screen. Pending HOD is a status badge as it appears in a list.
  • Procurement officer before a procedure names the role that performs it. Other roles do not see those buttons.
  • Numbered steps are done in order. The grey line after a procedure says what you should see when it has worked.
  • Every rejection, return, cancellation, waiver and override needs a reason. The reason stays on the record, in the audit log, and is shown to the person who raised the record.
Part 1

Launch

An organisation is live when real requisitions start in Procureline and the old paper process is kept only as a fallback. Launch runs in four stages behind one infrastructure gate. Nothing here is optional: each item is there because its absence costs real money.

1.1 The four stages

1.2 Prepare: the infrastructure gate

Nothing starts until these are done and signed off. Until two-factor sign-in is on, anyone who knows an email address and password can act as that person, Finance included. That is acceptable for testing and not for live purchasing.

Exit test. An administrator signs out, requests a password reset, receives the email, sets a new password, and signs back in with a real emailed code, at the organisation's own address.
Why the hourly jobs matter. The hosted API runs as serverless functions, which have no internal clock. Without the scheduler, nobody is reminded of overdue approvals, expiring supplier documents, low stock or an RFQ closing short of three quotes. The controls still hold (an expired required document blocks an award by itself) but the warnings do not go out.

Step 6 is the one most often skipped and the most expensive to find later. The system enforces the limit it is given, faithfully. If your real authority limits differ from the default of USD 5,000, it will enforce the wrong rule on real money.

1.4 Pilot and exit tests

Run the pilot with real people and real purchases in one or two departments for three weeks. The old process stays available as a fallback but is not run in parallel: doing both doubles the work and guarantees neither is trusted. The pilot has passed when all of these are true.

#Exit testEvidence
1At least ten real requisitions have reached paymentPayments list
2At least one order above the COO limit has passed every approval stageThe order's approval history
3At least one invoice has been correctly refused for goods not receiptedThe rejected invoice and its reason
4Stock figures agree with a physical countInventory against the count sheet
5Finance confirms the budget figures match its own recordsBudgets page
6No unresolved issue rated urgentThe issue list

Then hold a review with the results in front of you. Proceed, proceed after fixes, or stop. Write down which and why.

1.5 Cutover and fallback

Cutover is per department or siteA department switches when its rehearsal purchase passes. From that day, its new requisitions are raised only in Procureline.
In-flight work stays where it startedPurchases already under way finish in the old process. Moving half-finished purchases is more error-prone than letting them drain.
Fallback for one monthThe old process stays available for that department alone. The trigger is a defect that stops purchasing with no workaround within a day.
Decide and recordAfter each stage, review the exit-test results. Proceed, proceed after fixes, or stop. Write down which and why.

Part 2

Getting started

This part applies to everyone, staff and supplier alike: where to sign in, how the six-digit code works, how to recover a password, and how the screen is laid out.

2.1 Your organisation's address

Procureline lives at www.procureline.co.zw. Each organisation using it has its own address under that domain, and its data is kept apart from every other organisation's. Opening your address tells the system who you belong to. Your administrator gives you the address. A user of one organisation cannot sign in at another's address, and a supplier registers separately with each organisation it supplies. Where one address serves more than one organisation, the sign-in page adds a Business Unit list; choose yours there.

2.2 Signing in

Sign-in has two steps: your email and password, then a six-digit code sent to your email. The code proves that whoever has your password also has your mailbox.

  1. Open your organisation's address. The page says Sign in, with the line For group staff and registered suppliers. If a Business Unit list is shown, choose your organisation.
  2. Enter your Email Address and Password and click Continue. SHOW lets you check what you typed.
  3. Open your email. The page now says Enter verification code and names the address the code went to. The code has six digits and expires after 10 minutes.
  4. Type the code and click Verify & sign in. If it has expired, click Back to sign in and start again; a new code is sent.

You land on your Dashboard, with your name and role on the card at the top of the left-hand menu.

No code arrived? Check the spam folder, and check that the address on your user record is the one you are reading. Codes go to the address your administrator entered. If it is wrong, the administrator corrects it under Organisation › Staff Team; a supplier asks the buyer's Procurement team.

2.3 Forgotten password

  1. On the sign-in page click Forgot password?.
  2. Enter your Email and click Send reset link. The page says a link has been sent if an account exists; it does not reveal whether the address is known.
  3. Open the email and follow the link. The page Set new password asks for a New password and Confirm password, at least 6 characters.
  4. Click Update password, then sign in as usual. If the page says the link is invalid or incomplete, request a new one.

To change a password you still know, open your profile (2.4) and use Current Password, New Password and Confirm New Password. A supplier whose first password was generated by Procurement should change it at the first sign-in.

2.5 Your dashboard

The dashboard is the first page after sign-in and differs by role. It shows counts for your own pipeline, the items waiting on you under Needs attention, and Quick Actions. Two things are true for everyone: a count on the dashboard is a link to the list behind it, and an item appears in your queue only when it is at your step. If something you expect is missing, it is still with the person before you. Each role's part describes its dashboard.
