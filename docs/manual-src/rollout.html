Part 1

How it works

Every business unit runs on the same system and is kept completely separate from the others. The order of go-lives is chosen so that each wave proves something the next one depends on.

1.1 One address per business unit

ItemHow it works
AddressA subdomain of procureline.co.zw, for example fossil.procureline.co.zw. All fifteen addresses already answer over HTTPS
Sign-inThe address identifies the unit, so users never choose one. Email and password, then a six-digit code sent by email
DataEach unit has its own database. A script check proves that no unit can see another's records
RulesThe approval chain, the COO limit, the exchange rate and the supplier document rules are set per unit, so a unit's own delegation of authority is what the system enforces
SuppliersSuppliers register and quote through the unit's own address. A supplier that serves two units registers with each

1.3 Built-in controls

These apply in every unit from the day it goes live. They are the procurement procedure, enforced by the system rather than by memory.

ControlWhat it means
Three quotationsFewer than three needs a recorded waiver with a reason, approved by the COO
ApprovalsEvery purchase order is approved by the Head of Procurement, then Finance, and above the unit's limit (USD 5,000 unless set otherwise) by the COO
Three-way matchNo invoice is approved unless the order, the goods received and the invoice agree
Activated suppliersOnly a supplier with complete documents, checked by Procurement and approved by a second person, can be awarded work
Separate unitsEach unit's data is kept apart, and a session from one address is refused at another
Audit logEvery action is recorded with who did it and when, and cannot be edited

Part 3

What the system does

A short tour of the released system, so the reader of this plan knows what each unit is being given. The User Manual covers every screen step by step.

3.1 Signing in

Each unit signs in at its own address with email, password and a six-digit code sent by email. Suppliers register themselves through the same address.

3.2 Dashboards

Each role sees its own work: Procurement sees sourcing in progress, Finance sees what awaits approval, staff see their own requests and notifications.

3.3 Requisitions

Any member of staff raises a requisition. The department head approves it against the budget, Stores checks whether the items are in stock, and Procurement takes the rest forward.

3.4 Quotations and awards

Procurement publishes a request for quotation to activated suppliers, who respond online. Quotations are compared side by side; the award is authorised by the Head of Procurement, and fewer than three quotations needs a waiver.

3.5 Stores

Stores issues from stock where it can, records each delivery against the order, accepts or rejects it, and tracks stock across sites.

3.6 Invoices, payments and budgets

An invoice is paid only when it matches the order and the goods received. Spending is tracked against each department's annual budget as it is committed and as it is paid.

3.7 Suppliers

A supplier uploads its compliance documents through its own portal. Procurement checks them and a second person, the Head of Procurement or the COO, approves the activation. Expired documents block an award by themselves.

3.8 Administration and audit

Each unit's administrator manages users, departments, sites and the approval limit. Every important action is logged with who did it and when.

The festive period, 21 December to 1 January. The pilot falls across it on purpose: the units buy less, so a problem found then costs little, and the two live weeks before it already produce real purchases. During the break nothing new is set up, nobody else goes live, and the technical team is reachable by telephone. The pilot's third full week of purchasing is 4 to 8 January, and the exit tests count everything from 7 December.
Fallback. Each unit keeps its old process available for one month after its go-live, for that unit alone. Purchases already under way when it goes live finish in the old process; new requisitions are raised only in Procureline. The pilot units close their fallback at the go decision on 15 January.

4.2 One week of set-up, for every unit

The same week for every unit, pilot or wave. The unit's administrator coordinates; the owner column says who does each day. The steps are the launch checklist in the Launch Manual, 1.3.

DayTaskOwner
MondayCreate the unit's administrator; sites and departments, each with its head and deputy; users, one per role, each with a real email addressTechnical team, unit administrator
TuesdayConfirm the approval limit and the approval chain against the unit's own delegation of authority. Set the exchange rate for a rand unitFinance, COO

A unit whose rehearsal purchase fails on Friday does not go live on Monday. It repeats the failed step the following week and takes the next slot; the other units in its wave keep their dates.
