Section 02

Executive summary

Procureline is a web-based procure-to-pay and stores platform. It runs each purchase from the moment someone asks for something to the moment the supplier is paid, in one system with a full audit trail. It serves 15 business units, each on its own web address with its own database.

It replaces paper requisitions, emailed quotes and spreadsheet tracking. It also turns Tefoma's Central Procurement Procedure (Rev 9) into rules the software enforces, not guidance people can skip.

What it guarantees

Three quotations before an awardFewer is allowed only with a recorded waiver and a reason from a fixed list.
The right approvals, in orderA purchase order is approved by the Head of Procurement, then Finance, and also the COO at USD 5,000 or more.
No payment without receiptAn invoice is matched against the order and the goods received before Finance can approve it.
Only vetted suppliers are paidA supplier can quote while its Know Your Supplier (KYS) checks run, but cannot win an award or receive an order until they are complete.
Everything is on recordEvery create, approval, rejection, upload and sign-in is logged with the user, the time and the IP address.
Each unit stands aloneEvery business unit has its own address and database. A session in one unit is refused by all others.

Section 03

Why: background and objectives

Procurement ran on paper and email, so the controls in the written procedure were easy to work around. Procureline puts those controls in the path of the work, so the system refuses a step that breaks them.

The problems it was built to solve

#Problem beforeWhat the system does now
1No visibility: nobody knew where a requisition was without phoning aroundEvery request has a number, a status, an owner and a full history, visible in real time
2Duplicate buying: stores was skipped and items already held were bought againEvery approved requisition is checked against stock before Procurement can source it
3Weak sourcing: emailed quotes could be altered, lost or arrive after the decisionSuppliers quote inside the system; each version is locked and time-stamped
4Approval authority depended on who signed a paper, often out of orderThe approval sequence is enforced in code and cannot be skipped or reordered
5Invoices were paid without checking what was ordered or receivedA three-way match of order, goods received voucher (GRV) and invoice gates payment
6Audits meant searching physical filesEvery material action is logged with who, when and from where

Business objectives

IDObjectiveMeasure of success
BO-1Trace every purchase end to endAny request is found by number, with status and history, in seconds
BO-2Stop buying what is already heldEvery approved requisition passes a mandatory stores check
BO-3Enforce competitive, evidenced sourcingThree quotations per award, or a recorded waiver with a reason
BO-4Enforce delegated financial authorityApprovals run in a fixed order; the COO limit applies automatically
BO-5Prevent improper paymentNo invoice without an order; no approval without receipt and a three-way match
BO-6Trade only with vetted suppliersNo award or purchase order until the supplier's KYS is complete
BO-7Produce an audit trail without extra effortEvery material action is logged with actor, time and network address
BO-8Encode the Rev 9 procedure in softwarePolicy rules work as system constraints, not guidance
BO-9Serve the whole group with full separationEach of the 15 business units has its own address and database

Four-eyes controls. A supplier bank-detail change needs a call-back to a phone number already on file, and the approver must be neither the requester nor the person who made the call-back. An award needs one person to select it and a different person, the Head of Procurement, to authorise it.
Section 06

When: the end-to-end process

A purchase moves through 20 steps in five phases, and seven of them are controls the system enforces. Each step unlocks only when the one before it is complete.

Any approver can reject with a reason, which sends the record back with that reason on file. A requisition, RFQ or purchase order can also be cancelled with a reason from a fixed list.

Section 07

Approval matrix

Every approval belongs to one named role, runs in a fixed order and cannot be skipped. Purchase orders need two or three approvals, depending on the order's value in US dollars.

Purchase order approval chain (since 28 October 2026)

StageApproverRequired whenStatus while waiting
1Head of ProcurementAlwaysPending Head of Procurement
2Finance managerAlwaysPending Finance
3COOOrder total is at or above the unit's COO limit (default USD 5,000)Pending COO
  • Limit per unit. Each business unit has its own COO limit in US dollars, set by the administrator. The default is USD 5,000.
  • Other currencies. An order in another currency, such as ZAR, is converted to US dollars at the stored exchange rate before the limit is applied. The rate and limit used are saved on the order.
  • No exchange rate on file. The order goes to the COO, so an unknown conversion can never skip the highest approval.
  • Older orders. Orders submitted before 28 October 2026 may still show the old department-head stage. The Head of Procurement can approve them from there.

Section 09

Supplier lifecycle

A supplier can be asked for a price while its checks are in progress, because a quote commits nothing. It cannot win an award or receive a purchase order until its KYS is complete and it is marked spend-authorised.

Due diligence by tier

The depth of the Know Your Supplier (KYS) check depends on the supplier's risk tier. An unclassified supplier gets the full check, so low risk is never assumed by default.

TierMeaningKYS items requiredRe-evaluated every
CriticalSole source, or business stops if they failAll 16 required items3 months
StrategicHigh spend or long-term partnerAll 16 required items6 months
TacticalRoutine, bought competitively5: CR14, CR6, tax clearance, NSSA, insurance12 months
TransactionalLow value, low risk, easily replaced2: CR14, tax clearance24 months
UnclassifiedNot yet assessedAll 16 required items3 months

The full checklist has 20 items in seven groups: registration, tax, statutory compliance, licences, company capability, financial standing, and health, safety and environment.

Ongoing controls

  • Document expiry. Uploaded documents carry expiry dates. Reminders go out 60, 30 and 7 days before expiry.
  • Vetting. Procurement records site visits (date, who and notes), the vetting result, and how the supplier was found (newspaper advert, direct application, referral).
  • Bank details. A change is requested by the supplier, checked by a call-back to a phone number already on file, and approved by a third person.
  • Evaluation. After delivery, Procurement scores the supplier on 7 criteria: credit terms, contracts in place, market reputation, pricing, delivery, ease of dealing and consistent quality.
  • Ranking of quotations. Responses to an RFQ are scored out of 100: price 50, delivery 20, past performance 20, completeness 10. The ranking is advisory, and a snapshot is saved when the RFQ closes.

Section 11

Security, audit trail and notifications

Every user signs in with two factors, every request is checked against the user's role and business unit, and every material action is recorded. Controls are enforced on the server, so hiding a button is never the only protection.

Access control

ControlHow it works
Sign-inEmail and password, then a 6-digit code sent by email. The code expires after 10 minutes and allows 5 attempts
PasswordsStored only as bcrypt hashes; users reset their own through an emailed link
SessionsSigned tokens that expire after 7 days and are bound to one business unit
Supplier accessSeparate sign-in route; suppliers never reach internal screens or other suppliers' bids
Role checksEvery API route checks the role and, where relevant, the department or site. The administrator can read business records but not change them
Separation of unitsEach unit has its own database; a request without a known unit is refused
Sealed bidsQuotations cannot be compared until the RFQ closes
Bank detailsMasked on screen; changes need a call-back and an independent approver

Audit trail

Every create, update, delete, approval, rejection, submission, upload, download, status change, sign-in and failed sign-in is logged. Each entry records the user, their role, the record, the time, the IP address and the browser. Administrators can search the log, and an evidence pack endpoint gathers proof of controls for auditors.

Data retention

RecordKept for
Orders, invoices, payments, GRVs, quotations, RFQs, requisitions7 years from the end of the financial year they closed
Audit trail and bank-change records7 years
Active supplier profilesWhile the relationship lasts
Dormant or never-approved supplier profiles2 years without activity (7 years if they ever traded)
Staff accounts2 years after deactivation
In-app notifications12 months if read, 24 months if unread; deleted automatically
Sign-in codes1 day after expiry; deleted automatically

The retention policy is a draft awaiting approval by Finance and Legal.

Notifications

Users get in-app notifications, and email through Resend, when something reaches their step. Examples: a requisition to approve, a PO at their approval stage, a quotation selected for authorisation, an RFQ closing with fewer than 3 quotes, an over-budget approval, an invoice to match, low stock, expiring supplier documents and suppliers due for review.

Rollout plan

Assumptions

  • Every user, including each supplier contact, has an email address they can reach, because sign-in depends on an emailed code.
  • Stores keeps inventory in the system, not in parallel spreadsheets.
  • Each unit's COO limit and approval chain match its real delegation of authority. This must be confirmed per unit before go-live.
  • The group owns the source code, the data and every service account.

Constraints

  • Where the procedure and convenience conflict, the procedure governs, unless the business records a deliberate departure.
  • Sites have variable connectivity, so every screen must work in a mobile browser, approvals especially.
  • Suppliers are external, with no access to the company network and mixed technical skills.
