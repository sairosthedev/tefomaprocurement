Summary

At a glance

Procureline runs every purchase in your organisation from the request to the supplier's payment, with your procurement rules built in. This manual is for the people who bring it into use: what must be true before anyone buys through it, how to set the organisation up, how to pilot, and how to go live without losing a purchase on the way.

Part 1

Launch

An organisation is live when real requisitions start in Procureline and the old paper process is kept only as a fallback. Launch runs in four stages behind one infrastructure gate. Nothing here is optional: each item is there because its absence costs real money.

1.1 The four stages

1.2 Prepare: the infrastructure gate

Nothing starts until these are done and signed off. Until two-factor sign-in is on, anyone who knows an email address and password can act as that person, Finance included. That is acceptable for testing and not for live purchasing.

#TaskOwnerDone when
1The organisation owns its domain, its database account, its hosting account and its email-sending account. Nothing is on a personal accountIT, technical teamEach account is held by the organisation and has two administrators
2Database backups run daily with a copy kept elsewhere, and a restore has been performed into a scratch environment and timedTechnical teamA restore has actually been done, not only configured
3Procureline answers at the organisation's own address over HTTPSTechnical teamThe address opens the sign-in page with a valid certificate
4Real email sending is switched on, and the address used in email links is correctTechnical teamA notification email links to a working page
5Two-factor sign-in is on: a real six-digit code is required by emailTechnical teamSign-in cannot complete without the emailed code
6The hourly alert jobs are scheduled on the hosted APITechnical teamA scheduler calls the secured jobs endpoint every hour and the audit log shows it ran
7Suppliers imported from an older register have their permanent supplier codesTechnical teamEvery supplier in the list shows a code such as ORG-SUP-00001
8A separate test environment exists, so testing never touches live dataTechnical teamTesters sign in somewhere other than the live address
Exit test. An administrator signs out, requests a password reset, receives the email, sets a new password, and signs back in with a real emailed code, at the organisation's own address.
Why the hourly jobs matter. The hosted API runs as serverless functions, which have no internal clock. Without the scheduler, nobody is reminded of overdue approvals, expiring supplier documents, low stock or an RFQ closing short of three quotes. The controls still hold (an expired required document blocks an award by itself) but the warnings do not go out.

Step 6 is the one most often skipped and the most expensive to find later. The system enforces the limit it is given, faithfully. If your real authority limits differ from the default of USD 5,000, it will enforce the wrong rule on real money.

1.4 Pilot and exit tests

Run the pilot with real people and real purchases in one or two departments for three weeks. The old process stays available as a fallback but is not run in parallel: doing both doubles the work and guarantees neither is trusted. The pilot has passed when all of these are true.

#Exit testEvidence
1At least ten real requisitions have reached paymentPayments list
2At least one order above the COO limit has passed every approval stageThe order's approval history
3At least one invoice has been correctly refused for goods not receiptedThe rejected invoice and its reason
4Stock figures agree with a physical countInventory against the count sheet
5Finance confirms the budget figures match its own recordsBudgets page
6No unresolved issue rated urgentThe issue list

Then hold a review with the results in front of you. Proceed, proceed after fixes, or stop. Write down which and why.

1.5 Cutover and fallback

Cutover is per department or siteA department switches when its rehearsal purchase passes. From that day, its new requisitions are raised only in Procureline.
In-flight work stays where it startedPurchases already under way finish in the old process. Moving half-finished purchases is more error-prone than letting them drain.
Fallback for one monthThe old process stays available for that department alone. The trigger is a defect that stops purchasing with no workaround within a day.
Decide and recordAfter each stage, review the exit-test results. Proceed, proceed after fixes, or stop. Write down which and why.

Part 2

Administration

The administrator sets the organisation up in Procureline and keeps it tidy: sites, departments, users and their roles, and the audit log. The administrator cannot carry out any business step, which is what makes the audit log trustworthy.

Your dashboard. A system overview, recent activity, and attention rows for suppliers, KYS, evaluations due, low stock and failed sign-ins.

2.1 Sites

Create sites first, because departments, stock and deliveries refer to them. Open Organisation › Sites and click Add site: a short Code (such as HQ, locked once saved), the Type (HQ or Site), Name, city and province, and tick Has local store inventory for any site that holds its own stock. Stock transfers (User Manual 4.6) move stock between sites that have inventory.

2.2 Departments

Open Organisation › Departments and click Add Department: Department Name (required), a short Department Code, a description, the Department Head and a Deputy HOD. Both lists offer only users with the Department Head role, so create those users first (2.3) or come back and edit. The deputy can approve the department's requisitions when the head is away; name one for every department so approvals never stall. Create a department called Procurement: its head is the Head of Procurement.

2.3 Staff Team (users)

  1. Open Organisation › Staff Team and click Add User.
  2. Enter First Name, Last Name and Email. Use the person's real work address: sign-in codes and every notification go there, and the email cannot be changed later. Set a Password of at least 6 characters and confirm it; the user should change it at first sign-in.
  3. Choose the Role: Admin, Procurement Officer, Department Head, End User, Finance, COO or Stores Officer. Set the Department for everyone who raises or approves requisitions; a Department Head whose department is Procurement becomes the Head of Procurement, with full procurement access plus award authorisation and waiver approval. Add a phone number and leave Status as Active.
  4. Click Create User.

Toast User created successfully. The user signs in at your organisation's address (User Manual 1.2). To change a role or deactivate someone, click Edit; set Status to Inactive or Suspended rather than deleting, so their history stays attributed. Supplier logins are not created here; suppliers register themselves or are created by Procurement (User Manual 6.1).

One person, one role. Give nobody two logins to get around a control. The two-person rules (awards, bank changes, activations) assume the submitter and the approver are different people, and the audit log records the account, not the person at the keyboard.

2.4 Organisation settings

Open your profile from the card above the menu. The panel Business unit settings (Procureline's name for an organisation's settings) shows the organisation's code, country, base currency, the COO approval limit with the exchange rate it is applied at, and the document branding (legal name, VAT number, address, logo) printed on orders. These are read-only here; changes to the currency, limit, exchange rate or branding are made by the Procureline operators. Before launch, confirm the limit matches your delegation of authority (step 6 of the checklist). The warning No exchange rate on file — every PO routes to the COO means exactly that for an organisation whose currency is not USD.

2.5 Audit log and evidence pack

Oversight › Audit Logs records every sign-in, failed sign-in, create, update, delete, approval, rejection, submission, upload and download, with the user, role, time, IP address and the fields that changed. Search by description, user or IP; filter by action, entity and date. Click an entry for Audit Log Details, including before and after values. Export CSV exports the filtered list; Evidence pack produces the controls evidence for auditors for the date range (default the last twelve months). Check the log daily during the first weeks (1.7); the hourly jobs appear in it when they run.

2.6 Reports

Oversight › Reports shows spend trends, department spend and order value by status for the week, month, quarter or year, with Export to CSV. The Suppliers tab is the supplier registry with KYS, scores, orders and spend. Reports are available to every staff role except end users.

Part 3

Who does what

Eight roles use the system, and each sees and does only its own work. No single person can complete a purchase alone: the requester cannot approve, the approver cannot receive, and the receiver cannot pay.

Two-person rules. An award needs one person to select it and the Head of Procurement to authorise it. A supplier activation needs one person to submit it and the Head of Procurement or the COO to approve it. A bank-detail change needs a call-back by one person and approval by another, neither of them the requester.

Part 4

Help

Most problems are one of the following. Your first call is your administrator; a supplier's first call is the buyer's Procurement team. When you report a problem, give the record number (for example REQ-0042 or PO-0017) and what the screen said.

4.1 Common problems

I signed in but no code arrived.
Check spam. The code goes to the address on your user record; if that is wrong, the administrator fixes it under Staff Team. Codes expire after 10 minutes; click Back to sign in to get a new one. If nobody in the organisation receives codes, email sending has stopped: tell the technical team.
The page says my session is refused or sends me back to sign in.
You are at another organisation's address, or your session expired. Use your own organisation's address and sign in again.
I cannot see a button the manual describes.
Buttons appear only for the role that performs the step, and only when the record is at that step. Check the status badge: the record is still with the person before you. If your role is wrong, the administrator changes it.
My requisition has been "Pending HOD Approval" for days.
The department head has not acted. Their Approvals and Team Requisitions pages mark it overdue. If they are away, the administrator names a deputy on the department, who can approve.
I cannot submit my requisition.
It needs a title and at least one item. A new item not in the catalogue is fine; describe it.
Procurement cannot select or authorise my chosen supplier.
The supplier is not activated, its KYS is short for its tier, or a required document has expired. Open the supplier: the facts strip shows Spend authorisation and KYS progress. Complete the file and submit for activation (User Manual 6.5), or replace the expired document.
The RFQ closed with fewer than three quotes.
A waiver from the COO or Head of Procurement is needed (User Manual 5.5, User Manual 7.3), or re-source with more suppliers invited. Check that invited suppliers can actually sign in; help them register (User Manual 10.1).
The order went to the COO although it is small.
It is in a currency with no exchange rate on file, so the system routes it to the COO rather than guess. The Procureline operators set the rate (2.4).
Finance cannot approve an invoice.
Either there is no GRV for the goods (Stores must receive them first, User Manual 4.3), or the invoice exceeds what was received and not yet invoiced. Reject it so the supplier submits a corrected one.
A supplier says they changed their bank details but payments still go to the old account.
The change is waiting for the call-back or the second approval (User Manual 6.7). Until it is approved, the old account stands and payments are held.
Stock on the screen does not match the shelf.
A movement was not recorded. Find it in Stock Movements and record the missing issue, receipt or transfer. Do not edit the balance.
No reminders or overdue alerts are arriving.
The hourly jobs are not running on the hosted API. The administrator checks the audit log for the job and tells the technical team (1.2, item 6).
A supplier cannot register: "This company is already registered".
A record with that registration number exists, perhaps created by Procurement. The supplier signs in with that login or uses Forgot password?; otherwise Procurement looks the record up by registration number.

4.2 Glossary

TermMeaning
Activation requestA pending supplier put forward by Procurement for a second person to approve. Open until approved, returned or rejected
AwardThe decision to buy from a supplier's quotation, in full or line by line
Business unitWhat Procureline calls an organisation it serves: its own address, its own database and its own settings
COO limitThe order value in US dollars at or above which the COO must approve. The default is USD 5,000
CR14 / CR6Company registration forms: the list of directors (CR14) and the registered address (CR6)
Deputy headA user who can act for a department head on that department's requisitions

Head of ProcurementThe department head of the Procurement department. Authorises awards and gives the first approval on orders
IRInternal requisition: a requisition as seen by Stores during stock review
KYSKnow Your Supplier: the due diligence checklist a supplier must pass before it can be paid
MSDSMaterial safety data sheet, required for hazardous goods
NSSANational Social Security Authority; its compliance certificate is a KYS item
POPurchase order: the commitment to buy, sent to the supplier after approval
Requisition (PR)A request from a department for goods or services
Rejected (registration)A supplier registration turned down with a reason. The company cannot quote or be awarded; the registration can be reopened. Not a blacklisting
RFQRequest for quotation, sent to invited suppliers

Spend-authorisedA supplier whose activation has been approved and who may receive awards and orders
